A lead that fills out a form is ready to talk right now. By the time
someone spots the submission in a shared inbox or a CRM view, that
readiness is gone, and the deal dies quietly with no error to explain
why. Most teams lose inbound leads to exactly this gap. The plumbing
is slow, so the conversation never starts.

I built this engine to close that gap end to end. In a clean lab run,
a form submission became an owner's Slack alert in about two seconds,
with no manual step in between. The replay below shows a harder run,
with the enrichment provider rate-limiting the flow, and the lead still
reached its owner in under five seconds.

## How it works

**Intake.** The form posts to an n8n webhook behind header auth.
I chose to compute the idempotency key at the door, before enrichment
or scoring ever run, because a duplicate should be caught while it is
still cheap. Deduplicating later would burn an enrichment call and a
scoring pass on a lead the system already has. The trade-off: the key
comes from the raw submission, so a resubmit with edited fields counts
as a new lead. I accepted that, because dropping a real lead is worse
than an occasional near-twin.

**Persist first.** The raw payload is written to Postgres before any
processing starts. If a downstream step dies mid-run, the submission
still exists and can be replayed instead of being lost. That costs one
insert on the hot path and buys a hard guarantee: no lead that reached
the webhook ever disappears.

**Enrichment.** The enrichment call retries on failure, and when the
provider stays down the run does not fail. The flow falls back to the
firmographics the form supplied, marks the lead pending enrichment,
and keeps moving. In the run replayed below the provider answered 429,
the flow waited three seconds for it and then gave up, and the lead
still reached its owner in 4.77 seconds. Degraded data costs some
scoring precision. A stalled lead costs the deal.

**Scoring and routing.** Scoring is a config block of five explicit
rules, so the same input produces the same score on every run, and any
score can be explained by reading the rule list. Qualified leads then
resolve an owner: an account that already has one reuses it, and a new
account pulls the next rep from a round-robin pointer stored in
Postgres. The pointer lives in the database on purpose, so parallel
runs cannot grab the same slot and double-book a rep.

**CRM and alert.** The lead lands in HubSpot as a company and a
contact, found or created by domain so reruns never spawn duplicates.
Both HubSpot writes retry with exponential backoff and honor
Retry-After on 429s; after five attempts the flow records the failure
and moves on rather than blocking the alert. I wired the Slack message
through a Postgres outbox, so a failed post can be retried instead of
vanishing. The owner gets the lead with its score and company context
attached, and every lead, qualified or not, is recorded in Postgres
with its score and status. Nothing that came through the form goes
unworked.
