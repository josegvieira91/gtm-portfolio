This flow takes a prospect list, has a model draft one opening email per
person, and lets nothing reach the sender until the draft clears four
rules. In the run replayed below, 20 synthetic prospects went from a CSV
to my lab mailbox in 57.6 seconds.

A model will write a plausible cold email in a second, and it will just as
happily produce filler with the right shape and nothing inside it. My gate
cannot judge whether an email is good. It catches the failures that are
cheap to detect: wrong length, a stock opener, an unfilled token, a
missing company name, a pasted chunk of the prospect's row. That check,
and what happens when a draft fails it, is what I built.

## How it works

**Sourcing and drafting.** Apollo's free plan has no prospecting API, so
the flow reads a saved-search export off disk. This run used a synthetic
stand-in: 20 generated prospects on example.com, with Apollo's own column
headers. Addresses are masked on the way in. The model sees the prospect's
name, title, company, industry, headcount, location, website and a
one-line description, all from the row. Nothing is scraped.

**The gate.** The rules: 25 to 60 words, no stock opener or template
token, the company named, no more than 15 words copied in a row. A
rejected draft goes back with the broken rule attached, twice at most,
then lands in a review queue. The prompt carries the same rules, so a
first-pass clear is expected: all 20 cleared, at 31 to 44 words. The
drills are what show the gate refusing. In execution #135 I told the
writer to open with a stock line, skip the company and stay under 20
words. The gate refused all 60 attempts and nothing reached the sender. In
#134 every second prospect got those rules: 10 handed over, 10 in review.

**Outbox first.** Each approved draft is stored as pending before the send
runs. A message id from Gmail flips it to sent, an error to send_failed
with the text. In #136 I gave the send step an address with no domain. It
refused all 20, and every draft was still in the table.

**What sent means.** Handed to a mailbox I own. A node before the sender
replaces every destination with the lab inbox and throws if that address
is missing or off an allowlist. Nothing reaches a prospect, and in this
run there is no prospect to reach.

**One prospect per pass.** The loop took five at first. The mixed batch
(#133) showed the problem: a redraft finishes later than a clean pass, so
one batch re-entered the loop several times. Now each pass takes one
prospect and pauses a second, 20 seconds of the run.

**What this run does not prove.** The prospects are synthetic, so it says
nothing about reply rates or how a real buyer reads the drafts. It is 20
rows and one sender, a lab Gmail account rather than a warmed sending
domain.
